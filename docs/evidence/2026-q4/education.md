## Notes

- **Teachers.** Newer surveys of U.S. K-12 teachers find higher use: [NPR/Ipsos](https://www.ipsos.com/en-us/teachers-concerned-about-impact-ai-students-critical-thinking) 62% (Apr 27-May 5, 2026, 545 teachers), Gallup-Walton Family Foundation 60% (spring 2025, 2,232 teachers), and 58% of math and science teachers on RAND's American Teacher Panel (spring 2026). Switching from OECD's international lower-secondary sample to a U.S. K-12 sample would add about 25 points to the indicator through a population change rather than a change in use, so it is deferred to the 2027 methodology review.
- **Students.** Common Sense Media/NORC (spring 2026) reports 70% of U.S. teens using AI tools for schoolwork, but it counts any AI tool, including writing assistants, and part of the sample is nonprobability; not comparable with Pew's chatbot measure.
- **AI-Graded Assessments.** The held 8% has no traceable source, and no source found measures the same construct.
- **Turnitin.** Newer releases give U.S.-only breakdowns (higher education 19.4%, K-12 6.3%, Oct 2025-Apr 2026), not the all-market figure.

## Candidates For Methodology v3

- Pew: 10% of teens do all or most of their schoolwork with chatbot help (student delegation depth).
- Gallup-Walton: 16% of teachers use AI to grade at least monthly; 14% for one-on-one instruction or tutoring.
- State assessments: Texas, Massachusetts, Ohio, and New Jersey score most written responses by machine, with human checks on a share (for example at least 25% in Texas). There is no national total, and the published figures are design rules, not observed shares ([Texas scoring process](https://tea.texas.gov/data-reports/staar/scoring-process-staar-constructed-response-1.pdf)).
- Anthropic's education report: 48.9% of educators' grading conversations on Claude were automation-heavy (mid-2025), platform telemetry: <https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-education-report-how-educators-use-claude>.
